# Research, October 2026: self-transversal designs, `ε(n)`, FK-A's subproblems, substitution

Results beyond the thesis, in five tracks: A, the Witt design and self-transversal hypergraphs;
B, the frequency `ε(n)`; C, FK-A's recursion tree against its subproblems; D, substitution;
E, the bridges to `openai/math`. Item labels (A1, B2, …) are those of `thesis/CLAIMS.md`; §7
lists what is open. A *Theorem*, *Proposition*, *Lemma* or *Corollary* is proved in Lean for
every input, unless it is marked *conditional* or *cited*. An *Observation* is a finite fact:
proved in Lean by evaluation (`native_decide`, which trusts the Lean compiler as well as the
kernel) or by kernel `decide` when so marked, or measured in Python or Rust when so marked. A
*Conjecture* is stated with its evidence and search bound. Lean names refer to
[`lean/HypergraphDualization/`](../lean/HypergraphDualization); `lake build` in `lean/` checks all
of them, and `Axioms.lean` checks that each general theorem uses only the standard axioms and each
evaluation is one. Vertices are 0-indexed.

Notation. `U` is the ground set, `H` a hypergraph inside `U`, `Tr(H)` its minimal transversals.
`H` is *intersecting* if any two distinct edges meet, *2-colourable* on `U` if some `A ⊆ U` has
no edge inside `A` and none inside `U \ A`.

## 1. The Witt design `S(4,5,11)` (Track A)

## 2. Self-transversal hypergraphs

**Proposition 2.1** (A3). Let `H` be Sperner with every edge inside `U`. Then `Tr(H) = H` iff
`H` is intersecting, `∅ ∉ H`, and `H` is not 2-colourable on `U`.
Lean: `tr_eq_self_iff`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2.1.1 with `G = H`, `Tr(H) = H` iff for every `A ⊆ U` exactly one of `A` and
`U \ A` contains an edge. "At most one" for all `A` is the conjunction of: `H` intersecting
(take `A = e`) and `∅ ∉ H` (take `A = ∅`). "At least one" for all `A` is non-2-colourability. ∎

The Sperner hypothesis is needed: `H = {{0}, {0,1}}` on two points satisfies the right side but
`Tr(H) = {{0}}` (`tr_eq_self_iff_needs_sperner`).

**Theorem 2.2** (A4). Let `H` be a nonempty, intersecting, `r`-uniform hypergraph (`r > 0`) inside
`U`, `|U| = t`, in which every point of `U` has degree `d`. Then
`d(r² − t) ≥ r(r − 1)`, with equality iff distinct edges meet in exactly one point.
Lean: `intersecting_regular_bound`.

*Proof.* Let `b = |H|`. Counting incidences, `br = td`. Fix `e ∈ H`. Counting pairs `(x, f)` with
`x ∈ e ∩ f`, `rd = Σ_{f∈H} |e ∩ f| = r + Σ_{f≠e} |e ∩ f|`, and each of the `b − 1` terms is at
least 1. Hence `d(r² − t) − r(r − 1) = r(Σ_{f≠e}|e ∩ f| − (b − 1)) ≥ 0`, with equality iff every
term is 1. ∎

Self-transversal uniform regular hypergraphs are intersecting, so the bound applies to them;
`steiner_selftr.py` finds equality on `K₃` and the Fano plane, strict inequality on `w11`,
`Maj₅`, `Maj₇`, `Maj₉`. The step from equality to "projective plane of order `r − 1`" is not
proved here.

**Definition 2.3.** `H` is *read-once* on `V` if it is Sperner, *conformal* (every nonempty
clique of the primal graph lies in an edge), and its primal graph is a cograph, given by a
cotree on `V`: a single point, or a disjoint union or join of two cographs.
Lean: `ReadOnce`, `IsCograph`, `Conformal`.

This is Gurvich's characterisation taken as the definition. §4 also uses read-once *formulas*
(`Fm.ReadOnce`: no constants, disjoint inputs at every gate). A read-once formula on all of `V`
has a read-once DNF in the sense of Definition 2.3 (`Fm.readOnce_dnf`); the converse is Gurvich's
theorem, cited, not proved.

**Lemma 2.4.** Let a group act primitively on `α` and preserve a relation `R`. Then either the
equivalence closure of `R` is all of `α × α`, or `R u v` implies `u = v`.
Lean: `connected_or_trivial`.

*Proof.* The classes of the equivalence closure are blocks, since `g` maps the class of `v` onto
the class of `gv`. Primitivity makes each class a singleton or all of `α`. ∎

**Proposition 2.5** (A7). Let `H` be read-once on `n ≥ 2` points with `Aut(H)` primitive. Then
`Aut(H) = Sₙ`.
Lean: `aut_eq_top_of_readOnce`.

*Proof.* The root of the cotree is a union or a join of nonempty parts `A`, `B`. The primal graph
`P` and its complement are `Aut(H)`-invariant. *Union:* no edge of `P` crosses `A | B`, so the
closure of `P` does not join `A` to `B`; by Lemma 2.4, `P` has no edges. Every edge of `H` has at
most one point, and by transitivity a singleton edge forces every singleton, so every permutation
preserves `H`. *Join:* the same argument applied to the complement of `P` makes `P` complete.
Conformality puts `V` inside an edge, and Sperner gives `H = {V}`. ∎

**Corollary 2.6.** `w11` is not read-once: `Aut(w11)` is primitive of order `7920 ≠ 11!`. The
Fano plane is not read-once: its primal graph is complete, and a conformal Sperner hypergraph
with complete primal graph is `{V}` (`eq_univ_of_complete`).
Lean: `w11_not_readOnce`, `fano_not_readOnce`.

### Steiner systems

A *Steiner system* `S(t, k, v)` on `U`, `|U| = v`, is a family of `k`-subsets of `U` (blocks) such
that every `t`-subset of `U` lies in exactly one block. Lean: `IsSteiner`.

**Lemma 2.7.** In `S(t, k, v)`, an `i`-set with `i ≤ t` lies in `λ_i` blocks, where
`λ_i · C(k−i, t−i) = C(v−i, t−i)`.
Lean: `steiner_count`. (Double-count pairs `(T, e)`, `S ⊆ T ⊆ e`, `|T| = t`.)

**Lemma 2.8** (inclusion–exclusion). For a family `F` and a set `e`,
`#{f ∈ F : e ∩ f = ∅} = Σ_{S ⊆ e} (−1)^{|S|} #{f ∈ F : S ⊆ f}`.
Lean: `card_disjoint_eq`.

**Theorem 2.9** (A5a). In `S(t, k, v)` with `1 ≤ t ≤ k`, every block is disjoint from exactly
`n₀(t, k, v) = Σ_{i<t} (−1)^i C(k, i) (λ_i − 1)` blocks. Hence a Steiner system exists only if
`n₀ ≥ 0`, and one with `n₀ > 0` is not self-transversal.
Lean: `steiner_disjoint`, `steiner_n0_nonneg`, `steiner_tr_ne`.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 2.8 to a block `e`. A subset `S ⊆ e` with `|S| < t` lies in `λ_{|S|}`
blocks; one with `|S| ≥ t` contains a `t`-set and lies only in `e`. Grouping by `|S|` and using
`Σ_{i ≤ k} (−1)^i C(k, i) = 0` gives `n₀`. A self-transversal family is intersecting
(Proposition 2.1). ∎

**Observation 2.10** (A5b). `n₀` takes the values

| `(t,k,v)` | (2,3,9) | (2,3,13) | (2,3,15) | (3,4,8) | (3,4,10) | (3,4,14) | (3,4,16) | (5,6,12) | (5,8,24) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `n₀` | 2 | 10 | 16 | 1 | 3 | 20 | 39 | 1 | 30 |

| `(t,k,v)` | (2,6,16) | (2,6,21) | (2,12,45) | (2,10,46) | (2,10,55) | (4,12,57) | (2,15,85) | (2,14,92) | (2,20,96) | (2,12,100) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `n₀` | −5 | −5 | −22 | −18 | −18 | −195 | −42 | −39 | −57 | −22 |

so no Steiner system with the first nine parameter sets is self-transversal (this covers, for
example, all 80 systems `S(2,3,15)` and every `S(3,4,16)` at once), and none with the last ten
exists. Lean: `n0_table`, `steiner_tr_ne_of_table`, `not_steiner_of_table` (kernel `decide`).

**Observation 2.10a.** `PG(2,4)` is a Steiner system `S(2,5,21)` and `S(4,7,23)` (the weight-7
words of the binary Golay code) is a Steiner system `S(4,7,23)`; both are intersecting, and
neither is self-transversal: `{0,1,3,4,5,6,8,16}`, resp. `{0,1,2,4,5,6,7,8,9,11,12}`, meets every
block and contains none.
Lean: `pg24_steiner`, `pg24_intersecting`, `pg24_tr_ne`, `s4723_steiner`, `s4723_intersecting`,
`s4723_tr_ne`; the Steiner property via `isSteiner_of_packing` (a `k`-uniform family whose distinct
blocks share fewer than `t` points, with `b·C(k,t) = C(v,t)`, is `S(t,k,v)`).

The parameter sets `2 ≤ t < k < v ≤ 100` with `n₀ = 0` (`python experiments/steiner_selftr.py`)
are the projective planes `S(2, q+1, q²+q+1)`, `q ≤ 9`; `S(4,7,23)`; and `S(t, t+1, 2t+3)` for
even `t ≤ 44` with admissible parameters. Of the systems built there, `PG(2,3)`, `PG(2,4)` and
`S(4,7,23)` are 2-colourable (Python scan; in Lean as `pg23_not_self_dual` and Observation 2.10a).

**Theorem 2.11** (A5c). Let `H` be a Steiner system `S(t, t+1, 2t+3)`. Then `t` is even and
`Tr(H) = H`.
Lean: `witt_tr_eq`. The Fano plane is `t = 2`, the Witt design `w11` is `t = 4`.

*Proof.* (1) For `|S| = i ≤ t`, Lemma 2.7 and `C(n, m+1)(m+1) = C(n, m)(n − m)` give
`(t+3) · #{blocks ⊇ S} = C(2t+3−i, t+1−i)`, the number of `(t+1)`-subsets containing `S`.
Applying Lemma 2.8 to the blocks and to all `(t+1)`-subsets of `U`, which differ only at
`S = B`, gives `(t+3)·#{blocks disjoint from B} = (t+2)(1 + (−1)^{t+1})`. For odd `t`,
`t+3` would divide `2(t+2)`, which is impossible; for even `t` no two blocks are disjoint.
(2) No `(t+2)`-set contains two blocks, since they would share `t` points. So the `(t+2)`-sets
containing a block number `b(t+2)`. They are distinct from the `b` complements of blocks by (1),
and `b(t+3) = C(2t+3, t+2)`. Hence every `(t+2)`-set contains a block or is the complement of
one. (3) Let `|Y| ≥ t+3` contain no block. Then a `(t+2)`-subset `S ⊆ Y` is `U \ B` for a block
`B`. Take `x ∈ Y \ S`. For `y ∈ S` the set `S − y + x` is the complement of a block containing
`(B − x) + y`. Two choices of `y` give two blocks through the `t`-set `B − x`, a contradiction.
(4) One of `A`, `U \ A` has at least `t+2` points, so by (2)–(3) one of them contains a block:
`H` is not 2-colourable. Proposition 2.1 concludes. ∎

*Literature.* This is the classical family `S(m−1,m,2m+1)`, `m=t+1`, equivalent to perfect
1-codes in the odd graph `O(m+1)`; the Fano and Witt examples appear in
[Godsil 1995, §9, p. 13](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v2i1f1/pdf/).
This connection is cited, not formalised. It gives an older context for Theorem 2.11;
unsuccessful keyword searches do not establish novelty. The odd-`t` case may also follow
from the standard divisibility conditions. Novelty is not claimed.

**Conjecture 2.12** (A5d). A self-transversal Steiner system `S(t, k, v)` with `2 ≤ t < k < v` has
`k = t+1` and `v = 2t+3`. *Evidence:* for `v ≤ 100`, every admissible parameter set other than
`S(t,t+1,2t+3)` has `n₀ ≠ 0`, except the projective planes and `S(4,7,23)`. Projective planes of
order `≥ 3` have blocking sets containing no line ([Bierbrauer 1981, p. 85](https://www.numdam.org/item/RSMUP_1981__65__85_0.pdf), cited), and `S(4,7,23)` is unique
(Witt, cited) and 2-colourable (Observation 2.10a). The trivial family `S(k,k,2k−1)` (the
majority function) is self-transversal and is excluded by `t < k`.

### Criticality

A hypergraph is *3-critical* if it is not 2-colourable but becomes 2-colourable on deleting any
edge. For an intersecting Sperner `H` without the empty edge, 3-critical is the same as
self-transversal (Eiter, Makino and Gottlob 2008, §1; here Proposition 2.1 and Lemma 2.14).

**Theorem 2.13** (Seymour 1974; Bretto, *Hypergraph Theory*, Thm 3.6). A 3-critical hypergraph
covering `n` points has at least `n` edges.
Lean: `card_le_of_critical`.

*Proof.* Suppose `|H| < n`. The `n` incidence columns `x ↦ ([x ∈ e])_{e∈H}` lie in `ℚ^H`, so
some `y ≠ 0` has `Σ_{x∈e} y_x = 0` for every edge `e`; an edge carrying a nonzero entry carries
entries of both signs. Let `e₀` carry one and let `A'` 2-colour `H − e₀`. Put `x` in `A` if
`y_x > 0`, or if `y_x = 0` and `x ∈ A'`. Edges with a nonzero entry meet `A` and its complement;
the others lie in `H − e₀` and inherit the colouring of `A'`. So `H` is 2-colourable. ∎

**Lemma 2.14.** A self-transversal Sperner `H` is 2-colourable after deleting any edge `e`, by the
partition `(e, U \ e)`: no other edge lies inside `e` (Sperner) or avoids it (intersecting).
Lean: `erase_twoColourable`.

**Corollary 2.15.** A self-transversal Sperner hypergraph covering `n` points has `|H| ≥ n`.
Equality holds for `K₃` and the Fano plane; `w11` has 66 edges on 11 points.
Lean: `card_le_of_tr_eq_self`.

In the language of distributed systems ([`applications.md`](applications.md)), a non-dominated coterie on `n` nodes has
at least `n` quorums.

**Proposition 2.16** (counting test). Let `H` be Sperner inside `U`, intersecting, without the
empty edge. Then `Tr(H) = H` iff exactly `2^{|U|−1}` subsets of `U` contain an edge.
Lean: `tr_eq_self_iff_card`.

*Proof.* `A ↦ U \ A` maps the sets containing an edge bijectively onto the sets whose complement
contains one. The two families are disjoint because `H` is intersecting, and by Proposition 2.1
`Tr(H) = H` iff they cover the power set. ∎

This is the test of Mezzini et al. (arXiv:2505.09642, Lemma 4) for self-dual functions:
`f ≤ f^d` and `|f⁻¹(1)| = 2^{n−1}`. It is the counting form of the identity
`P_{1/2}(G) + P_{1/2}(Tr G) = 1` in `Connections`.

**Proposition 2.17.** If every minimal transversal of `H` is an edge (for example `Tr(H) = H`)
and `Aut(H)` is transitive on edges, then `Aut(H)` is transitive on the points `H` covers.
Lean: `transitive_of_edge_transitive`.

*Proof.* Let `O` be the orbit of `x`. Moving an edge through `x` onto any edge `f` puts a point of
`O` in `f`, so `O` is a transversal and contains a minimal one, which is an edge `e`. Moving `e`
onto an edge through `y` puts `y` in `O`. ∎

The Fano plane and `w11` are edge- and point-transitive; Proposition 2.17 says that for
self-transversal hypergraphs the first property implies the second.

## 3. The least most-frequent-vertex frequency `ε(n)` (Track B)

`ε(G, H) = max_v max(deg_G v/|G|, deg_H v/|H|)` for a transversal pair, both sides nonempty and
without the empty edge; `ε(n)` is its minimum over pairs on `n` points.

**Theorem 3.1** (B2). Let `(G, Tr G)` be a non-trivial transversal pair on `n` points,
`ε = ε(G, Tr G)`, and `s` the size of a smallest edge of `G`. Then `s·ε ≥ 1` and `s ≤ n·ε`.
Hence `n·ε² ≥ 1`.
Lean: `eps_bound`, `eps_sq`.

*Proof.* Each vertex has `deg_G v ≤ ε|G|`, so `|G|·s ≤ Σ_{g∈G} |g| = Σ_v deg_G v ≤ nε|G|`. Let `g`
be a smallest edge. Every minimal transversal meets `g`, so
`|Tr G| ≤ Σ_{h} |g ∩ h| = Σ_{v∈g} deg_{Tr G} v ≤ s·ε|Tr G|`. ∎

**Corollary 3.2** (Observation 5.2.3 settled). `ε(7) = 3/7`, attained by the Fano plane. Also
`ε(5) = ε(6) = 1/2` and `ε(9) = 1/3` (three disjoint triples).
Lean: `eps_seven`, `eps_five`, `eps_six`, `eps_nine`.

*Proof.* If `ε < 3/7` on seven points, then `s ≤ 7ε < 3`, so `s ≤ 2` and `sε < 6/7 < 1`,
contradicting Theorem 3.1. The other cases are the same argument; `ε(9) ≥ 1/3` is `9ε² ≥ 1`. The
attaining pairs are evaluated (`native_decide`). ∎

So `ε(n) ≥ L(n) := min_{s ≥ 1} max(1/s, s/n)`. Together with the attaining instances:

| `n` | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `L(n)` | 2/3 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 3/7 | 3/8 | 1/3 |
| `ε(n)` | 2/3 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 3/7 | `[3/8, 2/5]` | 1/3 |
| attained by | `K₃` | `{{0,3},{1,2}}` | same | same | Fano | 8-Ver (`2/5`) | three disjoint triples |

The bound is not tight in general: `PG(2,3)` has `ε = 112/247` against `L(13) = 4/13`
(`python` check), and at `n = 8` the solver evidence below puts `ε(8)` at `2/5 > 3/8`.

**Conjecture 3.3** (solver evidence). `ε(8) = 2/5`.

*Evidence.* `python experiments/eps_search.py` encodes "some pair on `n` points has every
frequency below `p/q`" as a 0/1 program on the truth table of a monotone `f : {0,1}ⁿ → {0,1}`
(`2ⁿ` variables, minterm indicators for `f` and its dual, degree ordering to break the `Sₙ`
symmetry) and solves it with OR-Tools CP-SAT:

| `n` | bound | CP-SAT | time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | `ε < 2/3` | infeasible | < 0.1 s |
| 4, 5, 6 | `ε < 1/2` | infeasible | < 0.1 s |

| 7 | `ε < 3/7` | infeasible | 0.1 s |
| 8 | `ε < 2/5` | infeasible | 646 s |

The rows for `n ≤ 7` agree with Corollary 3.2. The row for `n = 8` is CP-SAT's infeasibility
verdict, not a Lean certificate; Lean proves `3/8 ≤ ε(8) ≤ 2/5` (`eps_bound`, `eightVer_eps`).

### The gap and its equality case

**Theorem 3.4** (B4, excess and stability). Let `G,H` be nonempty cross-intersecting families
on `n` points, `a = |G|`, `b = |H|`, `ε = ε(G,H)`. Put
`X = Σ_{g∈G,h∈H} (|g ∩ h| − 1)`. Then

```text
ab + X = Σ_v deg_G(v) deg_H(v) ≤ n ε² ab.
```

Hence the fraction of cross pairs with intersection size at least two is at most `nε² − 1`.
One such pair gives `nε² ≥ 1 + 1/(ab)`. This needs neither Sperner nor duality.
Lean: `sum_degree_mul_eq_excess`, `eps_crossExcess`, `eps_badCross`, `eps_overlap_gap`.
With separate degree-frequency caps `p,q`, replace `ε²` by `pq` (`crossExcess_bound`).

*Proof.* Count triples `(v,g,h)` with `v ∈ g ∩ h`. Each cross pair contributes its mandatory
one point and its excess. Each vertex contributes at most `(εa)(εb)` triples. Every pair with
intersection size at least two contributes at least one to `X`. ∎

**Theorem 3.5** (B5, rigidity). Under the hypotheses of Theorem 3.4, if `nε² = 1`, then for
some positive integer `m`, `n = m²`, `mε = 1`, both families are `m`-uniform and Sperner, every
vertex has frequency `1/m` in each family, and every cross intersection is a singleton.
If `n` is not a square, `nε² > 1`.
Lean: `eps_eq_rigid`, `eps_eq_sperner`, `eps_eq_cross_singleton`, `eps_strict_of_nonsquare`.

*Proof.* Theorem 3.4 forces `X = 0` and equality in every degree product. Since the caps are
positive, each degree attains its cap. For any `g ∈ G`, summing its singleton intersections
with `H` gives `b = |g|εb`, hence `|g|ε = 1`. Interchange `G,H`, and use `nε² = 1`. Uniformity
implies the Sperner property. ∎

For a transversal pair, Gurvich's theorem now implies that equality forces a read-once
function. This last implication is **cited, not formalised**: [Das–Straßburger 2016,
Theorem 4.10](https://lmcs.episciences.org/2621/pdf). Thus a non-read-once transversal pair has
the quantitative gap of Theorem 3.4. In particular the regular convergent templates of
[`hard-classes.md`](hard-classes.md), (1.1), are read-once for every `r`; its searches at `r = 2,3`
are examples, not the boundary of the structural argument. Uniformity alone is insufficient:
`path_four_not_extremal` checks a 2-uniform pair on four points with `ε = 2/3` and `X = 1`.
This distinction leaves D3, which does not require regularity, open.

**Corollary 3.6** (B6, self-transversal gap). If `G = Tr(G)` is nonempty, then

```text
|G|² + Σ_{g∈G} (|g| − 1) ≤ n ε(G,G)² |G|².
```

If `n > 1`, then `nε(G,G)² > 1`. Lean: `eps_self_diagonal`, `eps_self_strict`.
The diagonal pairs supply the displayed correction. At equality, Theorem 3.5 and the diagonal
singleton intersections force `m = n = 1`. Fano has `X = 14` (`fano_crossExcess`), all from
diagonal pairs, and attains the excess inequality. The regular uniform special case is the
same incidence count as Theorem 2.2, not an independent discovery.

The degree-cap arguments are shared by `eps_bound` and Theorem 3.4. Likewise `eps_ge_inv`
proves `ε ≥ 1/m` whenever `m > 0` and `n ≤ m(m+1)`; the former separate proofs for `n = 5,6`
are now specialisations. The smallest-edge theorem remains useful because its integral `s`
can strengthen the square-root bound.

**Proposition 3.7** (B7, one regular side). Let `r > 0`, `n = r²`, and let cross-intersecting
families `G,H` both be `r`-uniform. If just `H` is regular, then every cross intersection
is a singleton. Lean: `uniform_regular_cross_singleton`.

*Proof.* Write `b = |H|` and `d = deg_H(v)`. Incidence counting gives `r²d = br`, hence
`rd = b`. For any `g ∈ G`, `Σ_{h∈H}|g ∩ h| = rd = b`; all `b` summands are at least one,
so they all equal one. ∎

Interchange the families to obtain the same result from regularity of `G`. For a transversal
pair, Gurvich's cited theorem therefore shows that a non-read-once candidate for D3 must
be irregular on **both** sides. This exclusion does not require frequency equality.

## 4. FK-A's recursion tree against its subproblem DAG (Track C)

For a dual pair, `τ` is the number of nodes of the modified FK-A recursion tree (`fkA`), and `δ`
the number of distinct Sperner-reduced pairs `(C, D)` among them (`fkADag`), i.e. the number of
subproblems a memoised FK-A solves.

**Lemma 4.1.** For any `G` and vertex `x`, `Tr(G/x) = Tr(G) − x` and `min(Tr(G)/x) = Tr(G − x)`.
Hence at a node `(C, Tr C)` split at `x`, the left child reduces to `(C − x, Tr(C − x))` and the
right child to `(Tr Q, Q)` with `Q = C/x`.
Lean: `tr_con_eq`, `minimal_con_tr`.

**Lemma 4.2.** The split rule returns a vertex of largest positive degree sum; every subproblem of
a pair supported on `V` is supported on `V`.
Lean: `splitVar_spec`, `fkADag_within`.

**Theorem 4.3** (C1). For the perfect matching on `v = 2m ≥ 4` points,
`τ = 2^{v/2+1} − 3` and `δ = v`. For the threshold graph on `v = 2k ≥ 2` points,
`τ = δ = v − 1`.
Lean: `matching_tau_delta`, `threshold_tau_delta` (general); `matching_eight`, `threshold_twelve`
(evaluation at `v = 8, 12`, agreeing with `fka.memo`).

*Proof (matching).* Induct on the set `S` of remaining pairs, `|S| = k + 1`, whatever pair FK-A
splits. Splitting at `x` in pair `i`: the left child is `M(S − i)`. The right child is
`(Tr Q, Q)` with `Q = {{x̄}} ∪ M(S − i)`, where `x̄` is the mate of `x`. There `x̄` has the unique
largest degree sum, since it lies in every transversal. Its left child is the leaf `(∅, {∅})` and
its right child is `M(S − i)` again. So `τ(k+1) = 1 + τ(k) + (2 + τ(k))`, which gives
`2^{k+2} − 3`. The new subproblems `(M(S), ·)` and `(Tr Q, Q)` use a vertex of pair `i`, so they
are absent from the subproblems of `M(S − i)` (Lemma 4.2). The leaf `(∅, {∅})` recurs, so
`δ(k+1) = δ(k) + 2`. *(Threshold.)* The top odd vertex `2k − 1` has the largest degree sum: it has
the most edges, and only `{0, …, 2k − 2}` among the minimal transversals avoids it, while every
other vertex is avoided by an explicit minimal transversal. The right child is that single-edge
leaf and the left child is the threshold graph on `2k − 2` points. Each step adds two new
subproblems. ∎

`matching` thus separates the tree from the DAG exponentially. `threshold` has no repeated
subproblem.

### Read-once pairs (C2)

Hertel (MSc thesis, Toronto 2004) settles the question for one branching order. DPLL simulates
FK-A (his Thm 4.1.7), and every DPLL decision tree for the alternating trees (the GK- and TK-families) has

left to right decides any read-once formula with `k` connectives in `2k + 3` nodes (Thm 4.4.1).
So C2 holds for that order, and the question left is FK-A's own rule: the vertex of largest
degree sum.

### Why the subproblems multiply

The thesis locates FK-A's cost in the number of subproblems and observes repeated ones (p. 53:
"The resulting contraction subhypergraph at Node 8 is identical to that at Node 17"). On a dual
input there are three separate sources of growth, and only the second is a repetition.

**Theorem 4.7** (normal form). For every `G`, split rule, fuel and variant, every subproblem of FK-A on
`(G, Tr G)` or on `(Tr G, G)` is `(H, Tr H)` or `(Tr H, H)`, `H` a Sperner-reduced minor of `G`
(iterated deletions `· − x`, contractions `· / x` and Sperner reductions). Hence
`δ ≤ 2·#{reduced minors of G}`.
Lean: `Minor`, `fkADag_minor`, `card_fkADag_le_minors`.

*Proof.* Induct on the fuel (`fkADagR_sub`: a set of pairs closed under both children contains
every subproblem). By Lemma 4.1 the children of `(G, Tr G)` at `x`, `G' = min G`,
reduce to `(G' − x, Tr(G' − x))` and `(Tr(G'/x), G'/x)`; those of `(Tr G, G)` are the same two
pairs in the other order. Apply the hypothesis to `G' − x` and `G'/x`. ∎

On functions, `G − x` is `f|x=0` and `G/x` is `f|x=1`. A subproblem is therefore a subfunction
`h = f|ρ` with a tag recording whether the pair is `(DNF h, DNF h^d)` or the reverse, and both
orientations of `h` have the children `h|x=0` and `h|x=1`.

**Proposition 4.8.** On a dual pair, `τ(C, D) = τ(D, C)`: the two orientations split on the
same vertex (`splitVar_comm`) into the same two children. Lean: `fkA_comm`.

**Theorem 4.9** (resilience). Suppose every minor of `G` by `k < d` deletions and contractions
keeps at least two edges and at least two minimal transversals. Then, with fuel at least `d`,
FK-A's tree on `(G, Tr G)` has `τ ≥ 2^{d+1} − 1` nodes, in either variant.
Lean: `tau_ge_of_resilient`.

*Proof.* Induct on `d`. At the root both sides have two edges, so a vertex has positive degree
and FK-A splits (`splitVar_spec`). The pair is dual, so the short-circuit never fires and
`τ = 1 + τ_L + τ_R` (`fkA_split`). Up to orientation (Proposition 4.8) the children are
`(H, Tr H)` for the minors `G' − x` and `G'/x`, which are `(d − 1)`-resilient. ∎

This is the growth of the tree, and no split rule escapes it; Hertel's DPLL lower bound (review
row 7) is the sharp form for the alternating trees. The hypothesis ranges over all minors,
constants included, so it is strong: contracting both points of one pair of `matching(2m)` gives
the constant `{∅}`, and `matching(8)` is not 3-resilient (`matching_eight_not_resilient`,
evaluation). For `m ≥ 3` the matching is 2-resilient and the theorem gives only `τ ≥ 7`; its
exponential tree, `2^{m+1} − 3` (Theorem 4.3), comes from the right child re-entering
`M(S − i)`, which Proposition 4.10 measures.

**Proposition 4.10** (overlap excess). Under any split rule, `δ ≤ τ` at every node, and at a
split of a dual Sperner node with children `L, R` and subproblem sets `Δ_L, Δ_R`,

```text
τ − δ ≥ (τ_L − δ_L) + (τ_R − δ_R) + |Δ_L ∩ Δ_R|.
```

Lean: `card_fkADag_le`, `excess_split`.

*Proof.* `τ = 1 + τ_L + τ_R` and `δ ≤ 1 + |Δ_L ∪ Δ_R| = 1 + δ_L + δ_R − |Δ_L ∩ Δ_R|`. ∎

This is the repetition the thesis saw. The work a memo saves is superadditive, and every split
whose children share subproblems adds their overlap. On `matching` the right child reaches the
left child again two levels down (`rnode` in `Dag.lean`), so `τ` doubles at each pair while `δ`
grows by two (Theorem 4.3).

**Observation 4.11** (the split rule; Python and Rust). The third source is FK-A's own rule. On
GK and TK the distinct subproblems number exactly `n` under the leftmost rule (`n − 1` on the
fan-in-3 alternating trees) and far exceed `N` under the degree-sum rule (Observation 4.4, last
two columns). Over 500 seeded random formulas
with fan-in up to 4 on at most 60 variables, leaves numbered left to right, the leftmost rule
gives `δ ≤ 1.07 n` and the degree-sum rule `δ/N` up to 25.4. Hertel's Theorem 4.4.1 (DPLLCache,
`2k + 3` nodes, cited) is the bound for the leftmost rule. (Python and Rust.)

The bound `δ ≤ 2n + 2` for the leftmost rule is now Theorem 4.16 (Lean). The degree-sum rule
picks the most frequent variable wherever it lies. It thereby interleaves disjoint subformulas,
and the subproblems become pairs of their partial states (Lemma 4.17): the product structure of
`ro12`.

**Remark 4.12.** A restriction of a read-once formula with nonempty support is determined by its
support: two reduced minors of `dnf f` with the same nonempty support are equal. A gate of the
restricted formula is live iff its subtree meets the support, and a constant input of a live gate
holds that gate's identity element. This is the encoding of `experiments/readonce_dag`, where a
node is `(S, tag)` in place of a formula.
Lean: `Fm.rminor_determined` (by induction on the formula, through Lemma 4.17).

So, to the thesis's question. The recursion tree is exponential because a dual node always
recurses into both children (Theorem 4.9). Most of the tree repeats subproblems, and
memoisation removes exactly that part (Proposition 4.10). What remains, `δ`, is small for a
coordinated split order and large for FK-A's most-frequent-vertex rule, because that rule
interleaves independent parts of the instance (Observation 4.11). ETH forces memoised FK-A to
be superpolynomial on some dual pairs (Proposition 4.6), a statement about `δ`. On the
alternating trees FK-A's own rule already shows superlinear growth of `δ`, in this
measurement.

### The memo DAG is a branching program

Modules `Algorithm`, `Dag`, `SplitRule`, `ReadOnceFormula`, `BranchingProgram`, `RuleInstances`.
`fkAR r` and `fkADagR r` are FK-A and its subproblem set under a split rule `r`; `fkA` and
`fkADag` are by definition the degree-sum instances (`fkAR_splitVar`, `fkADagR_splitVar`), and
the verdict is correct under every rule (`fkAR_correct`). A rule is *valid* if it returns a vertex of `support C ∪ support D`, and one
whenever that set is nonempty; `splitVar`, `leftRule` (the least live vertex) and `guardRule`
(Proposition 4.18) are valid and symmetric (`valid_splitVar`, `valid_leftRule`,
`valid_guardRule`).

A *branching program named by its functions* is a set `𝒮` of Sperner hypergraphs with a label on
each nonconstant `H ∈ 𝒮`: `H` tests `x ∈ support H`, its 0-successor is `H − x = f_H|x=0` and its
1-successor `min(H/x) = f_H|x=1`, both in `𝒮`; `∅` and `{∅}` are the sinks. Following labels
from `H` computes `f_H` (`run_correct`), and no path tests a variable twice (`path_nodup`). So `𝒮`
is a free BDD (FBDD, a deterministic read-once branching program) of size `|𝒮|`; under a static
order of the tested variables it is an OBDD. A family closed under a valid rule is such an FBDD
(`isFBDD_of_closed`).

**Theorem 4.13** (the memo DAG is an FBDD). Let `r` be valid and symmetric, `G` Sperner, and the
fuel larger than `|support G|`. The two sides of the subproblems of unmodified FK-A on
`(G, Tr G)`, with the constants, form an FBDD for `f_G` labelled by `r`, of size at most
`2δ + 2`. Under modified FK-A the same family is a branching program whose leaves are constants,
single terms and single clauses.
Lean: `memo_good`, `memo_fbdd`, `memo_bp`.

*Proof.* Every subproblem is a dual pair `(K, Tr K)` or `(Tr K, K)`, and with enough fuel it is a
leaf of the variant or has both children among the subproblems (`memo_good`; induction on the
fuel, the support shrinking at each split). An unmodified leaf has `|C||D| ≤ 1`, so it is a
constant or a literal `{{y}}`, which tests `y`. At a split of `(C, D)` at `x`, `C − x` and
`min(D/x)` are the sides of the left child and `D − x`, `min(C/x)` those of the right child; the
symmetric rule labels `C` and `D = Tr C` by the same vertex. ∎

**Proposition 4.14** (converse). An FBDD `𝒮 ∋ G` named by its functions gives a valid symmetric
rule `fbddRule` (test the label of whichever side of the node is a test node of `𝒮`, the lesser
label if both are, the degree-sum vertex if neither is) with `δ ≤ 2|𝒮|` on `(G, Tr G)`, either
variant. Lean: `fbddRule`, `rule_of_fbdd`.

So on unmodified FK-A the least `δ` over valid symmetric rules and the least FBDD of `f` named by
its functions agree within a factor 2 (Lean). The latter is the least FBDD of `f`: in an FBDD `B`
for a monotone `f`, each restriction `h` reached is computed by some node, and one with an
essential test is found by following 0-edges past inessential tests; labelling `h` by that test
gives a family of size at most `|B|`. (This reduction is argued here, not in Lean; review
row 29.)

**Corollary 4.15.** Under every valid symmetric rule, unmodified FK-A has
`δ ≥ (FBDD(f) − 2)/2`. A monotone family with `N` polynomial and FBDD size superpolynomial in `N`
would therefore be an explicit family of dual pairs defeating every rule, the open item of
Proposition 4.6. (For modified FK-A, each single-term or single-clause leaf expands to at most
`n + 1` nodes, so `δ ≥ (FBDD(f) − 2)/(2(n + 1))`; argued, not in Lean.)

*Literature.* Jukna, Razborov, Savický and Wegener (*Comput. Complexity* 8, 1999) separate
deterministic size from `NP ∩ coNP` for decision trees and read-once branching programs. Their
decision-tree separation, `N^{Ω(log N)}`, is the iterated NAND: the alternating trees, whose FBDDs
have at most `n + 2` nodes (Theorem 4.16, `Fm.leftmost_fbdd`). Their read-once separations are the pointer function, which is not
monotone, and the blocking-set function `⋀_lines ⋁_{j ∈ L} x_j` of a projective plane, which is
monotone but has every minimal blocking set as a term (Jukna, *Boolean Function Complexity*,
2012 draft, §14.9, §16.2, Exercise 16.1, read 2026-10-22). Neither gives small `N` with large FBDD
size. The standard criterion cannot give it either: an `m`-mixed function needs FBDD size `2^m`,
but a monotone `f` is not `m`-mixed for `m > log₂ N`, since by condition (iv) some term or clause
has at most `log₂ N` variables (`two_pow_shortest_le`), and two assignments to a set containing it
that fix it alike give the same constant subfunction. No monotone family with polynomial `N` and
superpolynomial FBDD size was found; one would need a lower-bound method other than mixedness.
Novelty of Theorems 4.13–4.14 is not claimed: that DPLL with caching yields FBDDs is standard in
knowledge compilation (Huang and Darwiche 2005, cited, not read).

### Split rules

**Theorem 4.16** (Observation 4.11; Hertel, Thm 4.4.1). Let `f` be a read-once formula with leaves
numbered left to right. Under the leftmost rule every subproblem of FK-A on `(dnf f, dnf f^d)` is
`(H, Tr H)` or `(Tr H, H)` with `H` a constant or one of at most `n` *states*, so `δ ≤ 2n + 2`,
for either variant and any fuel. The states and the constants, labelled by the leftmost rule,
form an FBDD for `f` with at most `n + 2` nodes.
Lean: `Fm.St`, `Fm.card_St_le`, `Fm.St_closed`, `Fm.leftRule_closed`, `Fm.leftmost_delta`,
`Fm.leftmost_fbdd`.

*Proof.* The states of `g ∘ h` (`∘` an AND or OR gate) are `A ∘ h` for the states `A` of `g`,
and the states of `h`; there are at most `|vars f|`. Since the leaves of `g` precede those of `h`,
the least vertex of `A ∘ h` is that of `A`, and restricting it gives `A' ∘ h` with `A'` a state or
a constant of `g`; `0 ∘ h` and `1 ∘ h` are constants or `h` itself (`St_closed`). So the states
and the constants form a family closed under the rule, and every subproblem lies over it
(`fkADagR_closed`). The constants contribute the two pairs `(∅, {∅})`, `({∅}, ∅)`. ∎

In branching-program terms, the read-once formula's OBDD in leaf order has at most one node per
variable besides the two sinks (`Fm.leftmost_fbdd`).
Fault-tree analysis uses this depth-first, left-most order for trees without repeated events
(Rauzy 1993, cited, not read). The identities behind the proof hold for every monotone formula:
`dnf(f|x=0) = dnf f − x`, `dnf(f|x=1) = dnf f / x`, `Tr(dnf f) = min dnf(f^d)`, and
`Tr(dnf f) = dnf(f^d)` for read-once `f` (`Fm.dnf_restrict_false`, `Fm.dnf_restrict_true`,
`Fm.tr_dnf`, `Fm.tr_dnf_readOnce`).

**Lemma 4.17** (interleaving). For any `A`, `B`, a minor of `A ∪ B` (of `A ∧ B`) is, as a
function, `A' ∪ B'` (`A' ∧ B'`) for minors `A'` of `A` and `B'` of `B`. On disjoint supports the
parts are recovered from the union, `(A₀ ∪ B₀) ∩ 2^V = A₀`, and from the join,
`{e ∩ V : e ∈ A₀ ∧ B₀} = A₀`.
Lean: `Fm.minor_union`, `Fm.minor_join`, `Fm.union_left_part`, `Fm.join_left_part`.

So below a gate on disjoint inputs the distinct subproblems are the distinct pairs of input
states the rule reaches, and `δ` is at least their number (argued from the lemma, not in Lean).

**Proposition 4.18** (a memo-friendly rule with FK96's guarantee). The guarded rule splits on the
least vertex `x` with `|C| ≤ s·deg_C x` or `|D| ≤ s·deg_D x`, `s = ⌊log₂ N⌋`, and on the
degree-sum vertex if there is none. It is valid and symmetric, so Theorem 4.13 applies to it. On
every dual pair with both sides nonempty it splits on a vertex of frequency at least `1/⌊log₂ N⌋`
on one side: the least such vertex, which exists by FK96 Lemma 2.3.2.
Lean: `guardRule`, `guardRule_symm`, `valid_guardRule`, `guardRule_frequent`.

So the frequency hypothesis of FK96's recursion analysis holds at every node of the guarded rule;
the analysis itself is not formalised here. Within the frequent vertices the rule follows a fixed

rows by `readonce_dag`, masks widened to 512 bits, which reproduces `δ(GK f₄) = 67,247,387`):

**Conjecture 4.19** (C2, the open part). Under FK-A's degree-sum rule, `δ/N` is unbounded on the

### All dual pairs (C3)

C3 as stated, `δ ≤ 2N` on all dual pairs, is refuted unconditionally by `ro12`
(Observation 4.5a), a read-once and hence dual pair. The polynomial form is refuted only
conditionally:

**Proposition 4.6** (conditional; cited). If the exponential-time hypothesis holds, then no polynomial
`p` satisfies `δ ≤ p(N)` on all dual pairs.

*Proof.* Each subproblem of memoised FK-A costs time polynomial in `N`. If `δ ≤ p(N)` held on dual
pairs, then running memoised FK-A and answering "not dual" as soon as it meets more than `p(N)`
subproblems would decide duality in polynomial time. Kobayashi, Kurita and Wasa
(arXiv:2610.11473, October 2026; preprint, not refereed) prove that under ETH duality has no
`N^{o(√(log N / log log N))}` algorithm. ∎

Their reduction is from 3-SAT and yields pairs with `L ⊆ Tr(H)`; it does not give an explicit
dual family with `δ/N → ∞`. The GK family under FK-A's own rule is now an explicit candidate:
`δ/N = 0.67, 1.92, 1368` for `k = 2, 3, 4` (Observation 4.4). Unboundedness is not proved, and
under the leftmost rule the same family has `δ = n`.

**Observation 4.20** (Track S; computed, 2026-10-23). Let `δ_iso` count the
isomorphism classes of ordered pairs reached by labelled memoised FK-A, and
`d_iso` the classes visited when an isomorphic cache hit stops expansion.
On GK `f₃` under modified FK-A with the degree-sum rule, `δ = 369`,
`δ_iso = 160` and `d_iso = 155`. The cache's split choices use vertex labels,
so its representatives can avoid classes present in the labelled DAG.

**Proposition 4.21** (Track S, relabelling foundation). For every permutation `σ`,
`σC = Tr(σD)` iff `C = Tr(D)`. Duality, including on redundant edge families,
is invariant under the same relabelling of both sides. Consequently the verdict
of `fkAR r` on `(σC, σD)` equals that of `fkAR s` on `(C,D)` for any two split
rules `r,s`, either variant and any fuel. This is a verdict statement; neither
the recursion tree nor its distinct-state count is claimed invariant.
Lean: `pimg_injective`, `tr_eq_pimg_iff`, `minimal_pimg`, `dual_pimg`, `fkAR_pimg`.

*Proof.* `Tr` commutes with relabelling (`tr_equivariant`), and relabelling is
injective. Applying `Tr` twice also makes Sperner reduction equivariant.
Use `Dual C D ↔ min C = Tr D`, then `fkAR_correct`. A formal cache implementation
and its invariant are still required for S1's algorithmic correctness claim.

**Theorem 4.22** (Track S, symmetry depth). Suppose `0 ≤ k ≤ n` and `Aut(G)`
is `k`-transitive. On `(G,Tr G)`, either FK-A variant under any live-vertex rule
has at most `2j` ordered isomorphism classes at each positive depth `j ≤ k`,
and at most `1+k(k+1)` through depth `k`. For `G=Tr G`, the bounds improve to
`j` and `1+k(k+1)/2`. No equivariance or side-symmetry of the rule is needed.
Lean: `SD.Trace.normal`, `SD.fkADag_trace`, `SD.hom_of_ktransitive`,
`SD.depth_bound`, `SD.self_depth_bound`, `SD.dual_transitive_bound`,
`SD.self_transitive_bound`; `SD.isoKey_eq_iff` identifies the quotient.

*Proof.* Every nonroot state has normal form
`(M(C,A,B),M(D,B,A))`, with the input sides possibly exchanged,
`A∩B=∅`, `|A|+|B|=j` and `A≠∅`. Both FK-A children insert their latest
split vertex into the first deletion set. Thus `|B|` has only `j` choices.
Transitivity identifies all states with the same source side and `|B|`;
equal input sides remove the source-side bit. Sum over depth. The
[full proof and scope](symmetry-depth.md) give the formal recursion bridge.

**Observation 4.23** (computed, 2026-10-23). Fano has layer counts `1,1,2`

### The recursion tree is a decision tree (Track T)

Modules `DecisionTree`, `FkaTree`, `GkMemo`. A decision tree reads one vertex per node; its size is its
number of leaves. Encode `true` as `−1` and write `f̂(S)` for the Fourier coefficients of `f` on
the subsets of the ground set. `coalt (2k − 1)` is GK `f_k` and `coalt (2k)` is TK `S_k`
(review row 1), on `n = 2^i` vertices.

**Theorem 4.24** (JRSW 1999, Lemma 2.2 and Theorem 2.4, for the alternating trees). Every decision
tree that computes `coalt i`, `i ≥ 2`, on the subsets of its `n = 2^i` vertices has at least
`2·(27/16)^{n/4}` leaves.
Lean: `l1_le_leaves`, `wc_gk_succ`, `dsum_succ`, `dsum_zero`, `dsum_eq`, `gk_leaves`.

*Proof.* A decision tree has at least `Σ_S |f̂(S)|` leaves (`l1_le_leaves`): at a node reading `x`
the coefficients split into the halves over `x ∉ X` and `x ∈ X`, each bounded by the `ℓ₁` norm of
one subtree, and a leaf has norm 1. Call `S` *dense* at level 2 if it is nonempty, and at level
`i + 1` if it is `S₁ ∪ (S₂ + 2^i)` with `S₁, S₂` dense at level `i`. A gate on the two copies has
`2 sg(a ∘ b) = ±1 + sg a + sg b ± sg a · sg b`; for nonempty `S₁, S₂` the first three terms have an
empty character sum, so the gate's coefficient at `S₁ ∪ (S₂ + 2^i)` is `±½ f̂(S₁) f̂(S₂)`
(`wc_gk_succ`). The dense sums therefore satisfy `c_{i+1} = c_i² / 2`, and `c_2 = 27/8` (kernel
evaluation by `decide`, `dsum_zero`), so `c_i = 2·(27/16)^{2^{i−2}}`. ∎

JRSW state the bound for the iterated NAND, which is `coalt` with complemented inputs; the Lean
proof works on `coalt` directly and rests on the standard axioms only.

**Theorem 4.25** (FK-A's recursion tree is a decision tree). Let `r` be a valid split rule and
`(C, D)` a dual pair on `n` vertices. FK-A's recursion tree on `(C, D)`, either variant, fuel
`n + 1`, unfolds into a decision tree for `f_C` with at most `(n + 1)·τ` leaves, `τ` the node
count of `fkAR`. Consequently, on GK and TK, under every valid rule,

    τ ≥ 2·(27/16)^{n/4} / (n + 1).

Lean: `fkDT`, `eval_fkDT`, `leaves_fkDT`, `fkA_tree_dt`, `fkA_gk_tree`; the degree-sum rule as
published is `fkA_gk_degree_sum`.

*Proof.* At a split of `(C, D)` at `x` the decision tree reads `x`. The left child `(C − x, D/x)`
carries `f_C|x=0` on its first side and the right child `(D − x, C/x)` carries `f_C|x=1` on its
second side; a tree asked for the second side uses the children the other way round. This holds
on every input and under every rule (`eval_fkDT`). On a dual pair both children are dual, FK-A
visits both, and every leaf has `|C| ≤ 1` or `|D| ≤ 1`; both sides are then constants, a single
edge or singletons (`dual_empty_left`, `dual_singleton_left`), decided by a chain of at most `n`
queries (`leaves_fkDT`). ∎

This makes Hertel's simulation of FK-A by DPLL (review row 7) exact, and replaces his bound
`n^{Ω(log^{1−o(1)} n)}` for DPLL trees on the alternating trees by `2^{Ω(n)}`. Measured
(`experiments/readonce_sim.py`, modified FK-A): at `n = 32` the bound is 4, while `τ = 66,913`
under the leftmost rule and `τ = 103,377` under the degree-sum rule. The bound is
`2.9·10⁵` at `n = 128` and `4.8·10²⁶` at `n = 512`.

**Corollary 4.26** (two separations on GK and TK).
(a) *Tree against DAG.* Under the leftmost rule `δ ≤ 2n + 2` (Theorem 4.16) while
`τ ≥ 2·(27/16)^{n/4}/(n + 1)`: memoisation saves a factor exponential in `n`. One Lean theorem
states both for the same pair: `coFm` writes `coalt` as an ordered read-once formula whose DNF is
`gkH` (`dnf_coFm_gkH`), so Theorem 4.16 applies to it (`gk_memo_gap`).
(b) *FK-A against FK-B.* On GK `f_k`, `N = 3·2^{2^k − 2}` and `n = 2^{2k−1} ≥ (log₂ N)²/2`, so
under every valid rule `τ ≥ N^{c log₂ N}/(n + 1)` with `c = log₂(27/16)/8 ≈ 0.094`. FK96 bound
FK-B's running time by `N^{4χ(N)+O(1)}`, `χ(N)^{χ(N)} = N`, which is `N^{o(log N)}`. FK-A's
running time on GK therefore exceeds FK-B's worst-case bound by a factor that tends to infinity,
under every valid rule. The crossover needs `log₂ log₂ N` above about 48 even with FK-B's `O(1)`
set to zero, so (b) says nothing about the measured ratios (52.96× on `gk-f3`). FK-B's bound is
cited, not formalised.

**Proposition 4.27** (C3 is a 1997 open problem). For monotone `f` the minimal DNF of `f` is its
set of minterms and that of `¬f` its set of maxterms, so the weight `‖f‖ = dnf(f) + dnf(¬f)` of
Jukna, Razborov, Savický and Wegener is `N = |C| + |Tr C|`. Their Question 1 (ECCC TR97-023,
§4) asks whether some Boolean function requires read-once branching programs (FBDDs) of size
superpolynomial in `‖f‖`. By Theorem 4.13 and Proposition 4.14, C3 for unmodified FK-A (an
explicit family of dual pairs with `δ` superpolynomial in `N` under every valid symmetric rule) is
equivalent, up to a factor 2 and the argued reduction of review row 29, to a positive answer to
Question 1 for monotone functions. A negative answer to Question 1 would refute C3
non-uniformly: every dual pair would have a rule with polynomial `δ`, without a way to find it.

*Proof.* The identification of `‖f‖` with `N` is the uniqueness of the minimal DNF of a monotone
function; the equivalence is Corollary 4.15 and Proposition 4.14. ∎

JRSW also ask whether the factor `log n` in the Ehrenfeucht–Haussler bound `exp(O(log n log² N))`
on decision-tree size can be removed. Theorem 4.24 shows that `N^{O(log N)}` would then be tight
on GK. For monotone `f`, a valid rule with `τ ≤ N^{O(log N)}` on every dual pair would give such a
decision tree (Theorem 4.25, and `n ≤ |C||Tr C| ≤ N²` because every vertex of a minimal minterm
is private to a maxterm). FK96's analysis gives `τ ≤ N^{O(log² N)}` for rules with their
frequency guarantee; between that and `N^{Ω(log N)}` the exponent of FK-A's worst case is open.
A bounded search on 2026-10-23 (the ECCC report read in full, three web searches) found no answer
to Question 1; Bollig (ECCC TR02-033) answers a different question from the same paper.

## 5. Substitution (Track D)

For a template `T` on points `ι` and hypergraphs `Γ i`, the substitution is
`T[Γ] = {⋃_{i∈t} g_i : t ∈ T, g_i ∈ Γ i}`: the composition `f_T(f_{Γ 1}, …, f_{Γ m})` of monotone
functions. Union and join are `T = {{0},{1}}` and `T = {{0,1}}`; the alternating trees and the word families
of `docs/hard-classes.md` are iterated substitutions.

**Theorem 5.1** (D1). For all `T` and `Γ`, `Tr(T[Γ]) = min(Tr(T)[Tr Γ])`. If the `Γ i` lie in
pairwise disjoint sets and are nonempty, the reduction is unnecessary:
`Tr(T[Γ]) = Tr(T)[Tr Γ]`. Without empty edges on disjoint supports, `T[Γ]` is Sperner when `T`
and every `Γ i` are, and `|T[Γ]| = Σ_{t∈T} Π_{i∈t} |Γ i|`.
Lean: `tr_subst`, `tr_subst_of_disjoint`, `subst_isSperner`, `card_subst`.

*Proof.* `X` hits `T[Γ]` iff the points `i` with `X` hitting `Γ i` form a transversal of `T`
(`hits_subst`; one direction picks, for a point `i` whose `Γ i` is missed, an edge of `Γ i`
disjoint from `X`). The same holds for "contains an edge" (`upHit_subst`). Two Sperner families
with the same up-sets are equal. On disjoint supports the part of `⋃ g_i` inside the support of
`Γ i` is `g_i`, which gives Sperner and the count. ∎

**Proposition 5.2** (D2). Let a template be `r`-uniform on `r²` points with `k` edges and `k*`
minimal transversals, both `r`-uniform, and satisfy `(P)`: `k·k* ≥ 8^{r−1}`. Substituting a copy of
an `s`-template with `(l, l*)` satisfying `(P)` into each point gives an `rs`-template with
`(k·l^r, k*·l*^r)`, which satisfies `(P)`. Under `(P)`, `c_∞ = max(log₂ k, log₂ k*)/(r − 1) ≥ 3/2`
(arithmetic, not in Lean).
Lean: `card_subst_uniform` (the counts, with `tr_subst_of_disjoint` on the dual side),
`prop_P_comp` (the inequality: `8^{rs−1} = 8^{r−1}·(8^{s−1})^r`).

**Open problem 5.3** (D3). Is there a prime template (not read-once), `r`-uniform on `r²` points
with an `r`-uniform dual, such that `k·k* < 8^{r−1}`? Candidates with either side regular are
excluded at every rank by Proposition 3.7 and the cited Gurvich consequence; the finite
regular classifications at `r = 2,3` are special cases. Both sides of a remaining candidate
must be irregular.

**Proposition 5.4** (D3 at `r = 2`). A nonempty 2-uniform family `G` on 4 points whose minimal
transversals are 2-uniform has `(|G|, |Tr G|) ∈ {(2,4), (4,2), (3,3)}`, so `k·k* ≥ 8`: the
answer to D3 at `r = 2` is no. Up to relabelling the three are the matching, the 4-cycle and the
path `P₄`. `P₄` is the prime one, with `k·k* = 9`.
Lean: `template_rank_two` (evaluation over the 64 families of pairs, `template_rank_two_check`).

At `r = 4`, `AG(2,4)`'s
parallel-class splits meet the necessary incidence conditions and fail duality. A search
over the transitive groups of degree 16 was not run.

## 6. Influences (Track E)

**Proposition 6.1** (E2). An automorphism `σ` of `G` preserves influences: at every density `p`,
the probability that `σ v` is pivotal for "contains an edge of `G`" equals that for `v`. Hence a
transitive `Aut(G)` gives all points the same influence; for the Fano plane all seven influences
agree at every `p`, and `P_{1/2} = 1/2` (`fano_k3_balanced`).
Lean: `influence_aut`, `influence_eq_of_transitive`, `fano_influence_eq`.

*Proof.* Reindex the sum defining the probability by `W ↦ σ(W)`. The product measure is invariant
(`bernoulliWeight_pmap`), `σ(insert v W) = insert (σ v) (σ W)`, and `σ(A)` contains an edge of
`G = σ(G)` iff `A` does (`upHit_pimg`). ∎

That `P_p` is strictly increasing for a nonconstant monotone property is Russo–Margulis, cited.

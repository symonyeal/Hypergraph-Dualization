Results beyond the thesis, in five tracks: A, the Witt design and self-transversal hypergraphs;
B, the frequency `ε(n)`; C, FK-A's recursion tree against its subproblems; D, substitution;
E, the bridges to `openai/math`. Item labels (A1, B2, …) are those of `thesis/CLAIMS.md`; §7

Notation. `U` is the ground set, `H` a hypergraph inside `U`, `Tr(H)` its minimal transversals.

## 2. Self-transversal hypergraphs

**Proposition 2.1** (A3). Let `H` be Sperner with every edge inside `U`. Then `Tr(H) = H` iff
`H` is intersecting, `∅ ∉ H`, and `H` is not 2-colourable on `U`.
Lean: `tr_eq_self_iff`.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2.1.1 with `G = H`, `Tr(H) = H` iff for every `A ⊆ U` exactly one of `A` and
`U \ A` contains an edge. "At most one" for all `A` is the conjunction of: `H` intersecting
(take `A = e`) and `∅ ∉ H` (take `A = ∅`). "At least one" for all `A` is non-2-colourability. ∎

The Sperner hypothesis is needed: `H = {{0}, {0,1}}` on two points satisfies the right side but
`Tr(H) = {{0}}` (`tr_eq_self_iff_needs_sperner`).

**Theorem 2.2** (A4). Let `H` be a nonempty, intersecting, `r`-uniform hypergraph (`r > 0`) inside
`U`, `|U| = t`, in which every point of `U` has degree `d`. Then
`d(r² − t) ≥ r(r − 1)`, with equality iff distinct edges meet in exactly one point.
Lean: `intersecting_regular_bound`.

*Proof.* Let `b = |H|`. Counting incidences, `br = td`. Fix `e ∈ H`. Counting pairs `(x, f)` with
`x ∈ e ∩ f`, `rd = Σ_{f∈H} |e ∩ f| = r + Σ_{f≠e} |e ∩ f|`, and each of the `b − 1` terms is at
least 1. Hence `d(r² − t) − r(r − 1) = r(Σ_{f≠e}|e ∩ f| − (b − 1)) ≥ 0`, with equality iff every
term is 1. ∎

Self-transversal uniform regular hypergraphs are intersecting, so the bound applies to them;
`steiner_selftr.py` finds equality on `K₃` and the Fano plane, strict inequality on `w11`,
`Maj₅`, `Maj₇`, `Maj₉`. The step from equality to "projective plane of order `r − 1`" is not
proved here.

**Definition 2.3.** `H` is *read-once* on `V` if it is Sperner, *conformal* (every nonempty
clique of the primal graph lies in an edge), and its primal graph is a cograph, given by a
cotree on `V`: a single point, or a disjoint union or join of two cographs.
Lean: `ReadOnce`, `IsCograph`, `Conformal`.

**Lemma 2.4.** Let a group act primitively on `α` and preserve a relation `R`. Then either the
equivalence closure of `R` is all of `α × α`, or `R u v` implies `u = v`.
Lean: `connected_or_trivial`.

*Proof.* The classes of the equivalence closure are blocks, since `g` maps the class of `v` onto
the class of `gv`. Primitivity makes each class a singleton or all of `α`. ∎

**Proposition 2.5** (A7). Let `H` be read-once on `n ≥ 2` points with `Aut(H)` primitive. Then
`Aut(H) = Sₙ`.
Lean: `aut_eq_top_of_readOnce`.

*Proof.* The root of the cotree is a union or a join of nonempty parts `A`, `B`. The primal graph
`P` and its complement are `Aut(H)`-invariant. *Union:* no edge of `P` crosses `A | B`, so the
closure of `P` does not join `A` to `B`; by Lemma 2.4, `P` has no edges. Every edge of `H` has at
most one point, and by transitivity a singleton edge forces every singleton, so every permutation
preserves `H`. *Join:* the same argument applied to the complement of `P` makes `P` complete.
Conformality puts `V` inside an edge, and Sperner gives `H = {V}`. ∎

**Corollary 2.6.** `w11` is not read-once: `Aut(w11)` is primitive of order `7920 ≠ 11!`. The
Fano plane is not read-once: its primal graph is complete, and a conformal Sperner hypergraph
with complete primal graph is `{V}` (`eq_univ_of_complete`).
Lean: `w11_not_readOnce`, `fano_not_readOnce`.

### Steiner systems

A *Steiner system* `S(t, k, v)` on `U`, `|U| = v`, is a family of `k`-subsets of `U` (blocks) such
that every `t`-subset of `U` lies in exactly one block. Lean: `IsSteiner`.

**Lemma 2.7.** In `S(t, k, v)`, an `i`-set with `i ≤ t` lies in `λ_i` blocks, where
`λ_i · C(k−i, t−i) = C(v−i, t−i)`.
Lean: `steiner_count`. (Double-count pairs `(T, e)`, `S ⊆ T ⊆ e`, `|T| = t`.)

**Lemma 2.8** (inclusion–exclusion). For a family `F` and a set `e`,
`#{f ∈ F : e ∩ f = ∅} = Σ_{S ⊆ e} (−1)^{|S|} #{f ∈ F : S ⊆ f}`.
Lean: `card_disjoint_eq`.

**Theorem 2.9** (A5a). In `S(t, k, v)` with `1 ≤ t ≤ k`, every block is disjoint from exactly
`n₀(t, k, v) = Σ_{i<t} (−1)^i C(k, i) (λ_i − 1)` blocks. Hence a Steiner system exists only if
`n₀ ≥ 0`, and one with `n₀ > 0` is not self-transversal.
Lean: `steiner_disjoint`, `steiner_n0_nonneg`, `steiner_tr_ne`.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 2.8 to a block `e`. A subset `S ⊆ e` with `|S| < t` lies in `λ_{|S|}`
blocks; one with `|S| ≥ t` contains a `t`-set and lies only in `e`. Grouping by `|S|` and using
`Σ_{i ≤ k} (−1)^i C(k, i) = 0` gives `n₀`. A self-transversal family is intersecting
(Proposition 2.1). ∎

**Observation 2.10** (A5b). `n₀` takes the values

| `(t,k,v)` | (2,3,9) | (2,3,13) | (2,3,15) | (3,4,8) | (3,4,10) | (3,4,14) | (3,4,16) | (5,6,12) | (5,8,24) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| `(t,k,v)` | (2,6,16) | (2,6,21) | (2,12,45) | (2,10,46) | (2,10,55) | (4,12,57) | (2,15,85) | (2,14,92) | (2,20,96) | (2,12,100) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `n₀` | −5 | −5 | −22 | −18 | −18 | −195 | −42 | −39 | −57 | −22 |

so no Steiner system with the first nine parameter sets is self-transversal (this covers, for
example, all 80 systems `S(2,3,15)` and every `S(3,4,16)` at once), and none with the last ten

**Theorem 2.11** (A5c). Let `H` be a Steiner system `S(t, t+1, 2t+3)`. Then `t` is even and
`Tr(H) = H`.
Lean: `witt_tr_eq`. The Fano plane is `t = 2`, the Witt design `w11` is `t = 4`.

*Proof.* (1) For `|S| = i ≤ t`, Lemma 2.7 and `C(n, m+1)(m+1) = C(n, m)(n − m)` give
`(t+3) · #{blocks ⊇ S} = C(2t+3−i, t+1−i)`, the number of `(t+1)`-subsets containing `S`.
Applying Lemma 2.8 to the blocks and to all `(t+1)`-subsets of `U`, which differ only at
`S = B`, gives `(t+3)·#{blocks disjoint from B} = (t+2)(1 + (−1)^{t+1})`. For odd `t`,
`t+3` would divide `2(t+2)`, which is impossible; for even `t` no two blocks are disjoint.
(2) No `(t+2)`-set contains two blocks, since they would share `t` points. So the `(t+2)`-sets
containing a block number `b(t+2)`. They are distinct from the `b` complements of blocks by (1),
and `b(t+3) = C(2t+3, t+2)`. Hence every `(t+2)`-set contains a block or is the complement of
one. (3) Let `|Y| ≥ t+3` contain no block. Then a `(t+2)`-subset `S ⊆ Y` is `U \ B` for a block
`B`. Take `x ∈ Y \ S`. For `y ∈ S` the set `S − y + x` is the complement of a block containing
`(B − x) + y`. Two choices of `y` give two blocks through the `t`-set `B − x`, a contradiction.
(4) One of `A`, `U \ A` has at least `t+2` points, so by (2)–(3) one of them contains a block:
`H` is not 2-colourable. Proposition 2.1 concludes. ∎

*Literature.* Searches on 2026-10-21 (Steiner systems `S(t, t+1, 2t+3)`, self-dual simple games
on Steiner systems, blocking sets) found no statement of Theorem 2.11. Systems `S(t,t+1,2t+3)`
are known for `t = 2, 4` only. The odd-`t` case may also follow from the standard divisibility
conditions. Novelty is not claimed.

### Criticality

A hypergraph is *3-critical* if it is not 2-colourable but becomes 2-colourable on deleting any
edge. For an intersecting Sperner `H` without the empty edge, 3-critical is the same as
self-transversal (Eiter, Makino and Gottlob 2008, §1; here Proposition 2.1 and Lemma 2.14).

**Theorem 2.13** (Seymour 1974; Bretto, *Hypergraph Theory*, Thm 3.6). A 3-critical hypergraph
covering `n` points has at least `n` edges.
Lean: `card_le_of_critical`.

*Proof.* Suppose `|H| < n`. The `n` incidence columns `x ↦ ([x ∈ e])_{e∈H}` lie in `ℚ^H`, so
some `y ≠ 0` has `Σ_{x∈e} y_x = 0` for every edge `e`; an edge carrying a nonzero entry carries
entries of both signs. Let `e₀` carry one and let `A'` 2-colour `H − e₀`. Put `x` in `A` if
`y_x > 0`, or if `y_x = 0` and `x ∈ A'`. Edges with a nonzero entry meet `A` and its complement;
the others lie in `H − e₀` and inherit the colouring of `A'`. So `H` is 2-colourable. ∎

**Lemma 2.14.** A self-transversal Sperner `H` is 2-colourable after deleting any edge `e`, by the
partition `(e, U \ e)`: no other edge lies inside `e` (Sperner) or avoids it (intersecting).
Lean: `erase_twoColourable`.

**Corollary 2.15.** A self-transversal Sperner hypergraph covering `n` points has `|H| ≥ n`.
Equality holds for `K₃` and the Fano plane; `w11` has 66 edges on 11 points.
Lean: `card_le_of_tr_eq_self`.

In the language of distributed systems ([`applications.md`](applications.md)), a non-dominated coterie on `n` nodes has
at least `n` quorums.

**Proposition 2.16** (counting test). Let `H` be Sperner inside `U`, intersecting, without the
empty edge. Then `Tr(H) = H` iff exactly `2^{|U|−1}` subsets of `U` contain an edge.
Lean: `tr_eq_self_iff_card`.

*Proof.* `A ↦ U \ A` maps the sets containing an edge bijectively onto the sets whose complement
contains one. The two families are disjoint because `H` is intersecting, and by Proposition 2.1
`Tr(H) = H` iff they cover the power set. ∎

This is the test of Mezzini et al. (arXiv:2505.09642, Lemma 4) for self-dual functions:
`f ≤ f^d` and `|f⁻¹(1)| = 2^{n−1}`. It is the counting form of the identity
`P_{1/2}(G) + P_{1/2}(Tr G) = 1` in `Connections`.

**Proposition 2.17.** If every minimal transversal of `H` is an edge (for example `Tr(H) = H`)
and `Aut(H)` is transitive on edges, then `Aut(H)` is transitive on the points `H` covers.
Lean: `transitive_of_edge_transitive`.

*Proof.* Let `O` be the orbit of `x`. Moving an edge through `x` onto any edge `f` puts a point of
`O` in `f`, so `O` is a transversal and contains a minimal one, which is an edge `e`. Moving `e`
onto an edge through `y` puts `y` in `O`. ∎

The Fano plane and `w11` are edge- and point-transitive; Proposition 2.17 says that for
self-transversal hypergraphs the first property implies the second.

## 3. The least most-frequent-vertex frequency `ε(n)` (Track B)

`ε(G, H) = max_v max(deg_G v/|G|, deg_H v/|H|)` for a transversal pair, both sides nonempty and
without the empty edge; `ε(n)` is its minimum over pairs on `n` points.

**Theorem 3.1** (B2). Let `(G, Tr G)` be a non-trivial transversal pair on `n` points,
`ε = ε(G, Tr G)`, and `s` the size of a smallest edge of `G`. Then `s·ε ≥ 1` and `s ≤ n·ε`.
Hence `n·ε² ≥ 1`.
Lean: `eps_bound`, `eps_sq`.

*Proof.* Each vertex has `deg_G v ≤ ε|G|`, so `|G|·s ≤ Σ_{g∈G} |g| = Σ_v deg_G v ≤ nε|G|`. Let `g`
be a smallest edge. Every minimal transversal meets `g`, so
`|Tr G| ≤ Σ_{h} |g ∩ h| = Σ_{v∈g} deg_{Tr G} v ≤ s·ε|Tr G|`. ∎

**Corollary 3.2** (Observation 5.2.3 settled). `ε(7) = 3/7`, attained by the Fano plane. Also
`ε(5) = ε(6) = 1/2` and `ε(9) = 1/3` (three disjoint triples).
Lean: `eps_seven`, `eps_five`, `eps_six`, `eps_nine`.

*Proof.* If `ε < 3/7` on seven points, then `s ≤ 7ε < 3`, so `s ≤ 2` and `sε < 6/7 < 1`,
contradicting Theorem 3.1. The other cases are the same argument; `ε(9) ≥ 1/3` is `9ε² ≥ 1`. The
attaining pairs are evaluated (`native_decide`). ∎

So `ε(n) ≥ L(n) := min_{s ≥ 1} max(1/s, s/n)`. Together with the attaining instances:

| `n` | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `L(n)` | 2/3 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 3/7 | 3/8 | 1/3 |
| `ε(n)` | 2/3 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 3/7 | `[3/8, 2/5]` | 1/3 |
| attained by | `K₃` | `{{0,3},{1,2}}` | same | same | Fano | 8-Ver (`2/5`) | three disjoint triples |

The bound is not tight in general: `PG(2,3)` has `ε = 112/247` against `L(13) = 4/13`
(`python` check), and at `n = 8` the solver evidence below puts `ε(8)` at `2/5 > 3/8`.

**Conjecture 3.3** (solver evidence). `ε(8) = 2/5`.

## 4. FK-A's recursion tree against its subproblem DAG (Track C)

For a dual pair, `τ` is the number of nodes of the modified FK-A recursion tree (`fkA`), and `δ`
the number of distinct Sperner-reduced pairs `(C, D)` among them (`fkADag`), i.e. the number of
subproblems a memoised FK-A solves.

**Lemma 4.1.** For any `G` and vertex `x`, `Tr(G/x) = Tr(G) − x` and `min(Tr(G)/x) = Tr(G − x)`.
Hence at a node `(C, Tr C)` split at `x`, the left child reduces to `(C − x, Tr(C − x))` and the
right child to `(Tr Q, Q)` with `Q = C/x`.
Lean: `tr_con_eq`, `minimal_con_tr`.

**Lemma 4.2.** The split rule returns a vertex of largest positive degree sum; every subproblem of
a pair supported on `V` is supported on `V`.
Lean: `splitVar_spec`, `fkADag_within`.

**Theorem 4.3** (C1). For the perfect matching on `v = 2m ≥ 4` points,
`τ = 2^{v/2+1} − 3` and `δ = v`. For the threshold graph on `v = 2k ≥ 2` points,
`τ = δ = v − 1`.
Lean: `matching_tau_delta`, `threshold_tau_delta` (general); `matching_eight`, `threshold_twelve`
(evaluation at `v = 8, 12`, agreeing with `fka.memo`).

*Proof (matching).* Induct on the set `S` of remaining pairs, `|S| = k + 1`, whatever pair FK-A
splits. Splitting at `x` in pair `i`: the left child is `M(S − i)`. The right child is
`(Tr Q, Q)` with `Q = {{x̄}} ∪ M(S − i)`, where `x̄` is the mate of `x`. There `x̄` has the unique
largest degree sum, since it lies in every transversal. Its left child is the leaf `(∅, {∅})` and
its right child is `M(S − i)` again. So `τ(k+1) = 1 + τ(k) + (2 + τ(k))`, which gives
`2^{k+2} − 3`. The new subproblems `(M(S), ·)` and `(Tr Q, Q)` use a vertex of pair `i`, so they
are absent from the subproblems of `M(S − i)` (Lemma 4.2). The leaf `(∅, {∅})` recurs, so
`δ(k+1) = δ(k) + 2`. *(Threshold.)* The top odd vertex `2k − 1` has the largest degree sum: it has
the most edges, and only `{0, …, 2k − 2}` among the minimal transversals avoids it, while every
other vertex is avoided by an explicit minimal transversal. The right child is that single-edge
leaf and the left child is the threshold graph on `2k − 2` points. Each step adds two new
subproblems. ∎

`matching` thus separates the tree from the DAG exponentially. `threshold` has no repeated
subproblem.

### Read-once pairs (C2)

Hertel (MSc thesis, Toronto 2004) settles the question for one branching order. DPLL simulates
FK-A (his Thm 4.1.7), and every DPLL decision tree for the alternating trees (the GK- and TK-families) has
`n^{Ω(log^{1−o(1)} n)}` nodes (Thm 4.3.6), so FK-A's recursion tree is superpolynomial on them
under every split rule. With memoisation (DPLLCache), branching on the leaves of the formula from
left to right decides any read-once formula with `k` connectives in `2k + 3` nodes (Thm 4.4.1).
So C2 holds for that order, and the question left is FK-A's own rule: the vertex of largest
degree sum.

(`word-yyy`), and has the same `δ`.

**Conjecture 4.5.** On read-once pairs FK-A's rule gives `δ ≤ 2N`. *Evidence:* `δ/N ≤ 1.93` on
every read-once pair above and in `docs/tree-vs-dag.md` §4. This would make memoised FK-A
output-polynomial on read-once pairs; it would not give Hertel's bound, which is polynomial in the
formula size `n` while `N` can be exponential in `n`.

### All dual pairs (C3)

**Proposition 4.6** (conditional). If the exponential-time hypothesis holds, then no polynomial
`p` satisfies `δ ≤ p(N)` on all dual pairs; in particular C3 (`δ ≤ 2N`) is false.

*Proof.* Each subproblem of memoised FK-A costs time polynomial in `N`. If `δ ≤ p(N)` held on dual
pairs, then running memoised FK-A and answering "not dual" as soon as it meets more than `p(N)`
subproblems would decide duality in polynomial time. Kobayashi, Kurita and Wasa
(arXiv:2610.11473, October 2026; preprint, not refereed) prove that under ETH duality has no
`N^{o(√(log N / log log N))}` algorithm. ∎

Their reduction is from 3-SAT and yields pairs with `L ⊆ Tr(H)`; it does not give an explicit
dual family with `δ/N → ∞`, which stays open. None of the families in this repository is a
candidate: their `δ/N` stays below 2.

## 5. Substitution (Track D)

For a template `T` on points `ι` and hypergraphs `Γ i`, the substitution is
`T[Γ] = {⋃_{i∈t} g_i : t ∈ T, g_i ∈ Γ i}`: the composition `f_T(f_{Γ 1}, …, f_{Γ m})` of monotone
functions. Union and join are `T = {{0},{1}}` and `T = {{0,1}}`; the alternating trees and the word families
of `docs/hard-classes.md` are iterated substitutions.

**Theorem 5.1** (D1). For all `T` and `Γ`, `Tr(T[Γ]) = min(Tr(T)[Tr Γ])`. If the `Γ i` lie in
pairwise disjoint sets and are nonempty, the reduction is unnecessary:
`Tr(T[Γ]) = Tr(T)[Tr Γ]`. Without empty edges on disjoint supports, `T[Γ]` is Sperner when `T`
and every `Γ i` are, and `|T[Γ]| = Σ_{t∈T} Π_{i∈t} |Γ i|`.
Lean: `tr_subst`, `tr_subst_of_disjoint`, `subst_isSperner`, `card_subst`.

*Proof.* `X` hits `T[Γ]` iff the points `i` with `X` hitting `Γ i` form a transversal of `T`
(`hits_subst`; one direction picks, for a point `i` whose `Γ i` is missed, an edge of `Γ i`
disjoint from `X`). The same holds for "contains an edge" (`upHit_subst`). Two Sperner families
with the same up-sets are equal. On disjoint supports the part of `⋃ g_i` inside the support of
`Γ i` is `g_i`, which gives Sperner and the count. ∎

**Proposition 5.2** (D2). Let a template be `r`-uniform on `r²` points with `k` edges and `k*`
minimal transversals, both `r`-uniform, and satisfy `(P)`: `k·k* ≥ 8^{r−1}`. Substituting a copy of
an `s`-template with `(l, l*)` satisfying `(P)` into each point gives an `rs`-template with
`(k·l^r, k*·l*^r)`, which satisfies `(P)`. Under `(P)`, `c_∞ = max(log₂ k, log₂ k*)/(r − 1) ≥ 3/2`.
Lean: `card_subst_uniform` (the counts, with `tr_subst_of_disjoint` on the dual side),
`prop_P_comp` (the inequality: `8^{rs−1} = 8^{r−1}·(8^{s−1})^r`).

**Open problem 5.3** (D3). Is there a prime template (not read-once) with `k·k* < 8^{r−1}`? For
`r ≤ 3` every convergent template is read-once (`docs/hard-classes.md` (1.3), exhaustive). At
`r = 4`, `AG(2,4)`'s parallel-class splits meet every necessary condition and fail duality. A
search over the transitive groups of degree 16 was not run.

## 6. Influences (Track E)

**Proposition 6.1** (E2). An automorphism `σ` of `G` preserves influences: at every density `p`,
the probability that `σ v` is pivotal for "contains an edge of `G`" equals that for `v`. Hence a
transitive `Aut(G)` gives all points the same influence; for the Fano plane all seven influences
agree at every `p`, and `P_{1/2} = 1/2` (`fano_k3_balanced`).
Lean: `influence_aut`, `influence_eq_of_transitive`, `fano_influence_eq`.

*Proof.* Reindex the sum defining the probability by `W ↦ σ(W)`. The product measure is invariant
(`bernoulliWeight_pmap`), `σ(insert v W) = insert (σ v) (σ W)`, and `σ(A)` contains an edge of
`G = σ(G)` iff `A` does (`upHit_pimg`). ∎

That `P_p` is strictly increasing for a nonconstant monotone property is Russo–Margulis, cited.
